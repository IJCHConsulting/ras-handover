## 1. Purpose

A discovery session to understand the RAS story, its market position and its brand ambitions, so Katie can develop a brand refresh and new website that reflect where the business is heading rather than where it started.

## 2. Business background and position

- RAS was set up by Sean and Billy after they had worked as project managers for QTS. It began as a simple labour supply business, earning a margin on supplied labour with no vans, kit or wider delivery responsibility.
- COVID was the step change. With reduced train running times, Network Rail maximised maintenance and pushed large volumes of work through QTS, at which point RAS was effectively delivering its entire workload for that single client.
- RAS began pricing and taking on packages of small schemes, worth a few thousand pounds each but available in high volume, keeping early risk low while it learned to deliver on price.
- It then invested in vans, its own kit and accreditations and moved up to larger schemes, most recently a 5.2 million pound scheme. RAS has invested heavily in specialist plant that significantly outperforms anything else the team had seen, which underpins both output and its edge on programme.
- Position today: a large subcontractor but a small principal contractor. Overheads mean it can no longer win the cheapest work, yet it is not scaled to compete head to head with the largest players such as BAM, Balfour Beatty, Murphy and QTS Group, who turn over roughly 200 million to 1.5 billion pounds.
- Ambition: secure a Principal Contractor Licence to work directly with Network Rail and win framework positions on RAS's own merit. The pre application audit was completed on the Monday before the meeting, and a relevant framework has recently come to market.
- Strategic focus: geotechnical rockfall mitigation and slope stabilisation, the specialism the kit is built for. Low value general and council work is to be avoided, as it does not make money, pulls the team off its specialism, and risks the reputation RAS is known for.

## 3. What RAS does

Captured in Sean's own words for use in the brand and copy:

- RAS stabilises rock cuttings and embankments, predominantly for transport networks, around 90 percent rail and 10 percent highway. In simple terms, this is drilling anchors and netting, either clearing a failure and pinning behind it, or catching a slope before it fails and pinning it.
- Related work includes badger exclusion and similar ecology tasks tied to the same embankments.
- A candidate positioning line emerged in the room: RAS prevents national disasters.

## 4. Brand personality and values

- Words Sean wants associated with RAS: loyalty, competence, honesty, driven, safe, positive mentality, and a strong culture and camaraderie that goes beyond ordinary teamwork.
- Core value: people first. In Sean's words, look after the people in the business and the business looks after itself. Leadership is approachable and hands on, so the team is comfortable flagging problems early rather than hiding them.
- A clear pursuit of excellence: a known standard the team works to, and a willingness to invest in the best kit and people for safety and output.

## 5. Current brand and website

- Sean values the existing logo, the blue, and the social impact side of the brand, from a council estate background through to sponsoring local football, motorbikes and athletes.
- The website is seen as adequate but uninspiring. It does not convey the size, scale or capability of the business, and currently acts as a landing point rather than a sales vehicle.
- Given the risk involved in the work, the digital presence needs to instil confidence from the outset and match the physical capability the team demonstrates on site. Sean's framing: this is about getting RAS into the room, after which the physical work speaks for itself.

## 6. Creative direction

- Colour: blue stays and is meaningful, linked to the Scotland flag. Orange has no sentimental tie and can change or be dropped. No green, by firm request. Katie to explore tones and a versatile palette.
- Logo: keep the heroic, action led feel, but simplify. The current emblem is too complex to scale cleanly, so the aim is a simpler, cleverer, more minimalistic mark that works across all applications, from officeware to social. Integrating the emblem imagery with the brand name is one avenue to explore.
- Reference sites Sean rates: CAN, for strong imagery and informative engineering and case study content, also blue and orange; QTS, for time lapse video; and SAS, for a clear, to the point layout. The overall aspiration is to present at the calibre of RAS's target clients.

## 7. Website structure, SEO and content

- Structure: two headline services, rockfall mitigation and slope stabilisation, supported by a capabilities page, and a dedicated engineering page. The engineering page should use the language buyers use, developed with input from Richard, including terms engineers expect such as BIM.
- Buyers to design for: procurement and supply chain managers, and engineering divisions within tier one and tier two contractors.
- SEO is a priority. RAS currently ranks poorly on Google for terms as niche as slope stabilisation, despite low competition. Blog and news are underused and are the primary route to improve ranking and draw traffic into the key service pages.
- Content: RAS holds case studies, photography, drone footage and time lapse video well beyond what the current site shows. This is a strength to build on. Social effort should concentrate on LinkedIn, where the buyers are.

## 8. Process and next steps

- Katie will research the industry and competitors, then issue a design direction document setting out two directions across colour, type and overall feel. This is not finished brand work. Sean's first task is simply to choose the direction that fits.
- Katie then develops a single full brand proposal, including the logo suite and real world mock ups, iterating from there if needed.
- Web design begins a couple of weeks later, once the brand direction is agreed. Simon and Katie will confirm the updated sitemap and content requirements, including the engineering page, before the build starts.
- Assets will be shared through a OneDrive or Dropbox folder set up between the three parties.
